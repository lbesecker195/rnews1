# Abbott freezes Texas Flock camera funding

Texas Gov. **Greg Abbott** ordered state agencies to pause funding for **Flock Safety's** AI-powered license-plate cameras as privacy backlash and alleged police misuse spread. The **Texas Tribune** said the order came as it prepared an investigation showing a state agency had put at least **$30 million** into the Flock network via local-police grants.

A pause is not a ban. It is a governor hitting freeze on a network that can already see a lot of Texas asphalt. Spokesman **Andrew Mahaleris** said most city camera money is *federal*, and Texas agencies are clarifying that *state* funds cannot be used for Flock. That split is the whole fight: who paid, who can look, and who gets to keep looking while Austin pretends the tap is off.

## A $1 fee that bought a surveillance web

The **Motor Vehicle Crime Prevention Authority**, with a board mostly appointed by Abbott, has helped install at least **3,200** Flock cameras since **2023**, including nearly **1,200** for **DPS** under a three-year, **$15.9 million** contract. The money came from a 2023 **$1** auto-insurance fee meant to fight catalytic-converter theft.

That origin story is doing a lot of work. A dollar on an insurance bill is easy to sell as a cop tool against chopped cars. Three thousand cameras that log make, model, color, and bumper stickers, shareable nationwide, is a different product. Converter theft is a real crime. A statewide, interoperable plate-reader mesh is a real surveillance system. Both can be true. Only one needs a pause.

## What the cameras actually log

Flock's pitch is not a blurry still of a plate. The cameras log **make, model, color and bumper stickers** and can be shared nationwide. Bumper stickers are the tell. That is not who sped through this intersection. That is which Subaru with the national-park decal went north at 2 a.m.

Once those hits live in a network other departments can query, the local justification (converters in Lufkin, a stolen truck in Houston) is no longer the limit of the search. The limit is whoever has a login and a reason they will type into a box.

## Lufkin is the misuse with a case number

A **Lufkin** officer faces **100 counts** for allegedly using Flock to surveil **11 people** for more than a year. That is not a hypothetical about mission creep. It is a charging document about a cop treating an anti-theft camera grid as a personal tracking service.

One hundred counts is how you know the alleged use was not a one-off lookup after a 911 call. Eleven people, more than a year: that is a pattern. Privacy backlash in Texas did not need a seminar. It needed Lufkin.

If a tool can be queried that way, someone will query it that way. The pause is Abbott reacting to the predictable use of a system his own appointees helped buy.

## Thirty million, and then a freeze

The Tribune's forthcoming investigation, as described, says a state agency put at least $30 million into the Flock network through local-police grants. Abbott's order lands on top of that reporting, not in a vacuum. Freeze first, argue later, is how you look like you discovered the bill.

Mahaleris pointing at federal dollars is a dodge with a footnote. If most *city* camera money is federal, a state pause does not unplug the city cameras. It stops the next state check. The 3,200 cameras already up, including nearly 1,200 for DPS, do not vanish because agencies are clarifying. They sit there, logging stickers, until someone turns them off or changes the sharing rules.

## Catalytic converters and the bait-and-switch

Converter theft exploded. Legislatures hunted a fee, a task force, a gadget. Flock was the gadget. AI plate readers are very good at noticing a car that should not be in a scrap-yard driveway. They are also very good at noticing a car that should not be at a clinic, a church, or an ex's street.

The $1 insurance fee was sold as a narrow tax for a narrow crime. The network it helped build is neither. Nationwide sharing is how a Texas converter program becomes a queryable map of movements for any agency that is allowed in.

A pause on state funding is the cheapest way to look like a privacy governor without immediately fighting cities, DPS, or the vendor. It is also, if it holds, a signal to the next grant writer: do not put Flock on the state line item.

## What a pause can and cannot do

It can stop new state money. It can force MVCPA and DPS to explain the $15.9 million and the 3,200 installs. It can give the Tribune's investigation a news peg besides the investigation itself.

It cannot, by itself, delete a year of bumper-sticker logs. It cannot finish the Lufkin case. It cannot make federal dollars in city budgets obey a Texas memo. Privacy people who wanted a kill switch will call this a press release. Police chiefs who wanted more cameras will wait out the clarification.

The honest read is in between. Abbott appointed most of the board that helped seed the network. He is now ordering the seed money paused because the cameras did what cameras do when you give them AI and a share button. Lufkin is the exhibit. Thirty million is the price. Make, model, color, stickers, nationwide: that is the product Texas bought to stop stolen converters.

State funds cannot be used for Flock, agencies are now supposed to say. The cameras already bought with the converter fee are still watching the road. The pause is about the next dollar. The argument is about the last 3,200.
The nationwide share button is the part that will not stay paused. A Lufkin lookup that can see a Dallas hit, or a Dallas lookup that can see a Lufkin bumper sticker, is why 3,200 cameras are not 3,200 local toys. They are one mesh. Abbott can freeze the next state dollar. He cannot, with a memo, make that mesh forget how to dial out. The Tribune's $30 million figure is the receipt for building it. The 100 counts in Lufkin are the receipt for using it. Catalytic-converter theft was the slogan that made the $1 fee feel small. The freeze is the admission that the slogan bought something bigger than stolen cats, and that Texas is not sure it wants to keep paying.
